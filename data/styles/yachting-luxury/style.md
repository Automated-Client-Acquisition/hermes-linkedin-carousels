# Style: yachting-luxury

Premium superyacht recruitment aesthetic — calm, exclusive, and quietly authoritative. Every slide must feel like a real physical nautical artifact: aged navigation charts, leather captain’s logs, brass-bound manifests, handwritten fountain-pen notes, wax seals, pinned documents, brass instruments, or ship’s papers. Not clean minimal design. Rich contextual decoration using actual yachting objects.

This file defines ONLY the brand-consistency layer. Composition, hero treatment, and decoration on each slide are decided contextually by the model based on the slide's content.

## When to use

- Luxury service positioning, recruitment, salary guides, and career advice in the yachting / private staff / superyacht world
- Founder or agency manifestos that need quiet confidence rather than loud marketing
- High-trust, high-ticket B2B or B2C content where exclusivity matters

DO NOT use for: mass-market consumer products, playful startup energy, technical SaaS dashboards, or anything that needs bright colors or heavy illustration.

## Palette

| Token          | Hex      | Role                              |
|----------------|----------|-----------------------------------|
| Deep Navy      | `#0A1F3D` | Dark hero / statement backgrounds |
| Warm Brass     | `#C5A26F` | Accent, rules, emphasis, CTAs     |
| Warm Bone      | `#F8F5F0` | Light content backgrounds         |
| Ink Navy       | `#151D2E` | Primary text on light             |
| Muted Sand     | `#8B7E6D` | Secondary text, captions          |

Color discipline:
- Brass (`#C5A26F`) is used sparingly — only on key phrases, thin rules, or small accents.
- Never combine Deep Navy and Warm Bone on the same slide without a strong brass separator.
- Dark slides use Deep Navy as the dominant background.

## Typography

| Element    | Treatment                                              |
|------------|--------------------------------------------------------|
| Headline   | Clean, slightly condensed sans-serif, 72–110px, Ink Navy or white. Brass used for one emphasized word or phrase per headline. |
| Body       | Readable sans-serif, 28–36px, Ink Navy on light or white on dark. Generous leading. |
| Kicker     | Small all-caps tracking, Muted Sand, 18–22px           |
| Page marker| Small brass or muted sand number, bottom right or left |
| Wordmark   | Small, elegant, always in Warm Brass or Deep Navy      |

## Voice and bans

- NEVER use gradients, drop shadows on type, or beveled effects
- NEVER use beachy, tropical, or tourist imagery (no palm trees, no obvious waves as decoration)
- NEVER use cheap luxury clichés (gold foil, excessive bling, diamond sparkles)
- NEVER use bright or saturated colors outside the locked palette
- NEVER use playful or casual language in visuals
- NEVER use stock photo clichés of smiling crew on deck unless the slide is specifically about people

Voice register: calm luxury, quietly authoritative.

## Realism

The universal realism rules in `references/designer-moves.md` apply.
For yachting-luxury specifically:

- Avatars: subtle, high-end portrait treatment or omitted entirely. Prefer silhouette or name-only when possible.
- Mockups: clean device or document previews with the locked palette only.
- Charts: minimal and elegant if used — brass lines on navy or bone.
- Brand marks: real logos when a brand is named. Pass the actual logo SVG as reference.

## Continuity chrome

- Thin brass horizontal rule (1–2px) used consistently as a separator or footer accent.
- Page number always appears in the same location and treatment.
- Brass is the only accent color allowed across the entire carousel.
