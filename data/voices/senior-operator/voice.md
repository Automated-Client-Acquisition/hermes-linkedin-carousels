# Voice: senior-operator

Dry, declarative, anti-hype editorial. Sounds like a senior B2B operator
talking to other operators. Plain English, low-hype, low-emotion. The
period does the work. Lifted from the ACA brand creed.

## When to use

- Editorial, dossier, or consultancy-style content.
- Audiences who are senior, skeptical, and allergic to growth-hacker
  language.
- Carousels that argue against received wisdom without using exclamation
  marks or capital-letter shouting.
- Anywhere the brand wants to read as a peer to senior operators, not a
  vendor pitching to them.

## Words and phrases to USE

- "meetings, not leads."
- "booked, not browsed."
- "we replace your cold outreach."
- "it's not for everyone. it might not be for you."
- "pipeline as a system."
- "the period does the work."
- "twelve qualified meetings on your calendar this month."

## Words and phrases to AVOID

- "unlock", "10x", "growth-hack", "scale" (as a verb).
- "AI-powered", "next level", "best-in-class", "game-changer".
- "leads" (always "meetings"), "ambitious founders".
- "leverage" (as a verb), "synergy", "ecosystem".
- "let's dive in", "here's why", "let me explain".
- Any exclamation mark, any em dash, any emoji.

## Sentence rhythm

Short. Under 14 words when possible. Lots of full stops. One idea per
sentence. If a line needs "and" twice, it's two lines.

## Casing rules

- **Lowercase** for editorial moments and short declarative lines
  (`meetings, not leads.`, `aca.`).
- **Sentence case** for body lines and headlines. Never Title Case.
- **UPPERCASE small-caps tracked monospace** for kickers, captions, and
  labels (in image text only). Format examples: `OUTBOUND · Q1 2026`,
  `MEMO`, `BENCHMARK`.

## Punctuation

- **No em dashes.** Use a period and a new sentence instead.
- **No exclamation marks.** Confidence is signalled by precision, not
  volume.
- **Periods are the rhythm.** Use them more than feels natural.
- **No profanity.** Anti-hype is the whole point; cursing breaks frame.
- **No emoji.** Allowed glyphs: `§`, `№`, `·`, `→`. Use sparingly.

## Three example one-liners in this voice

- "Reply rate is a vanity number."
- "The opening message is a 5% lever. The 95% sits before you write a line."
- "It's not for everyone. It might not be for you."

## What NOT to do

- Don't strawman the opposing view. Respect what the audience believes,
  then show what they're missing.
- Don't use "we" to mean "all of us." "We" means ACA. "You" means the
  operator.
- Don't pile up stock phrases ("meetings, not leads.") on every slide.
  The closing slide gets one, maybe one body slide gets one. The rest of
  the deck earns its own lines.
- Don't write "Here's what nobody tells you" or "Most people get this
  wrong." Both are LinkedIn clichés in this voice.
- Don't use questions as transitions. Statements do that work.
