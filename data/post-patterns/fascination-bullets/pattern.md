# Pattern: fascination-bullets

Brunson-style curiosity bullets that tease a benefit or reveal without
giving the answer. Each bullet creates an open loop the reader can only
close by engaging (commenting, DMing, clicking). The most powerful
pattern for lead-magnet posts and "here's what's inside" teasers.

## When to use

- The user has a real lead magnet (a doc, an audit, a system, a stack)
  the bullets describe.
- The CTA is `keyword-capture` — the bullets are the contents preview.
- The hook is any high-intent variant: `stop`, `never`, `rip`,
  `credibility`, `bold-number`.

## Structure

```
Setup (1-2 lines): what the doc/audit is and why now.

Bullets section header (1 line): "Inside:"

The bullets (4-7 lines, each one open loop, NO resolution):
→ [bullet 1, one of the 8 formulas below]
→ [bullet 2, different formula]
→ [bullet 3]
→ [bullet 4]
(stop at 4-7)

CTA setup (1-2 lines): "Want the doc?" or "Want the full system?"
```

## Worked example

```
I just packaged 6 months of cold email testing into one doc.

Inside:

→ The 3-word subject line that outperformed our best template by 4x
→ Why "personalization" is tanking your reply rates (counterintuitive but true)
→ The one metric we track that predicts if a campaign will convert
→ How we book 12+ calls/month from a list of 500 (not 50,000)
→ What nobody tells you about email warmup

Want the full doc?
→ Comment "COLD"
→ I'll send it over.
```

## The 8 fascination bullet formulas

Alternate formulas across bullets. Never use the same formula twice in
a row.

```
The Secret Of:   "The secret of [desirable outcome] (and why [obvious
                  approach] actually makes it worse)"

How To:           "How to [desired result] without [expected painful
                  requirement]"

The One Thing:    "The one [element] that [bold claim] (hint: it's not
                  [obvious assumption])"

Why:              "Why [common practice] is killing your [metric] and
                  what to do instead"

The X That:       "The [specific number]-word [element] that [outsized
                  result]"

What NEVER:       "What [authority/experts] will never tell you about
                  [topic]"

The Weird:        "The 'weird' [tactic/trick] that [unexpected result] (I
                  almost didn't try this)"

Warning:          "Warning: do NOT [common action] until you [specific
                  condition]"
```

## Rules

- 4-7 bullets. Three reads weak. Eight is over.
- Each bullet is ONE line, max 15-20 words. If it wraps to a third line
  on mobile, it's too long. Cut.
- NO explanations after the bullet. The bullet IS the line. No
  trailing clauses ("and how to automate the detection").
- Alternate formulas. Never the same structure twice in a row.
- Be specific: numbers, timeframes, concrete details where real.
- Information gap: tease the WHAT and the RESULT, withhold the HOW.
- Real bullets only. Don't promise content the doc doesn't contain.

## What NOT to do

- Don't resolve the curiosity inside the bullet. The whole point is the
  loop stays open.
- Don't run two ideas together. "The 3-word subject line that 4x'd
  reply rate AND how to automate it" is two bullets badly compressed.
- Don't use parentheticals to explain. Parenthetical asides for
  punctuation (max 5 words) are fine; parenthetical explanations are
  not.
- Don't fabricate the contents. If the doc doesn't have "the 3-word
  subject line that 4x'd our rate," don't write that bullet.

## Pairs well with

Hooks: `stop`, `never`, `rip`, `credibility`, `bold-number`,
`nobodys-talking`. CTAs: `keyword-capture` (the bullets are the doc's
table of contents, the comment is the gate) or `generosity-frame` (the
explicit "I'd charge for this, but here it is").
