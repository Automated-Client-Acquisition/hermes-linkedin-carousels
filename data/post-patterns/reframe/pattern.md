# Pattern: reframe

Take a commonly held belief and flip it to reveal what actually matters.
The body names the false belief, then walks the reader through the truer
mental model. Ends with the operational instruction the new frame implies.

## When to use

- The user has a clear "actually, here's what matters" thesis.
- The audience holds the false belief recognizably.
- The hook is `contrarian-punch`, `curiosity-gap-misdirect`, or
  `pattern-interrupt-question`.

## Structure

```
The wrong belief (1 line, stated flat).

Why the wrong belief looks right (1-2 lines).

The truer thing (1-2 lines).

The mechanism behind the truer thing (1-2 lines).

The instruction: what to do instead (1-2 lines, imperative).
```

## Worked example

```
Everyone obsesses over open rates.

But open rates are a vanity metric.

What matters is reply rate.
And reply rate is a function of relevance, not cleverness.

Stop writing "better" subject lines.
Start sending to better people.
```

## Rules

- Name the false belief in one line, flat. No "Some people might say..."
- The truer thing has to be defensible and operational. Not "what matters
  is mindset."
- The mechanism line earns the reframe. Without it, the post is just
  contrarianism.
- The closing instruction is imperative. "Stop X. Start Y." reads
  cleanly; "Maybe consider trying Y" doesn't.
- Real terms only. No invented metrics.

## What NOT to do

- Don't strawman. The false belief has to be one the audience actually
  holds.
- Don't pile on the false belief with two paragraphs of why it's stupid.
  One line stating it, one line on why it looks right, then move on.
- Don't end on "thoughts?" or a question. The instruction lands harder.
- Don't introduce a third position ("actually, both are wrong"). The
  pattern is binary by design.

## Pairs well with

Hooks: `contrarian-punch`, `curiosity-gap-misdirect`,
`pattern-interrupt-question`. CTAs: `mic-drop` (the instruction line IS
the mic drop), `bridge`, or `keyword-capture`.
