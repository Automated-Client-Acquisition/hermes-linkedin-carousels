# Pattern: story

A brief narrative with a clear operational lesson. One event, one
decision, one consequence, one takeaway. The shortest viable story is
usually the right length.

## When to use

- The user has a real event they can describe.
- The event teaches something the audience can use.
- The hook is `confession`, `contrast`, or `curiosity-gap`.
- The voice is `hype` or `clean`. `senior-operator` rarely tells stories.

## Structure

```
Event (1-2 lines): what happened. Concrete and dated.

Decision (1 line): the choice the user made.

Why the decision (1-2 lines): the principle behind it.

Consequence (1-2 lines): what happened next.

Lesson (1-2 lines): the operational takeaway.
```

## Worked example

```
Last month, a client asked me to "just set up the automations."

I said no.

Not because I couldn't.
Because automations without strategy are just expensive ways to annoy people faster.

We rebuilt their ICP from scratch first.
Then the automations wrote themselves.
```

## Rules

- The event has to be real. Truth rule applies. If the user didn't lose
  a client last Tuesday, don't write that they did.
- The decision is the spine of the post. Make it explicit.
- The "why" line is the lesson disguised as motivation. It does the
  teaching.
- The consequence is concrete. "It worked out" is not a consequence.
- Total length 80-200 words. Shorter is almost always better.

## What NOT to do

- Don't lead with the event date. The hook handles that. The body opens
  inside the event.
- Don't pad with sensory detail ("I was sitting at my desk, the rain
  was hitting the window..."). LinkedIn isn't a novel.
- Don't tag the client by name unless they explicitly approved it.
- Don't end on a generic moral ("trust the process"). The lesson is
  operational; "rebuild the ICP first" is a lesson, "trust yourself" is
  a fortune cookie.

## Pairs well with

Hooks: `confession`, `contrast`, `curiosity-gap`. CTAs: `mic-drop` or
`bridge`. Avoid `keyword-capture` on most stories — the tonal shift
from narrative to "comment a word" can undercut the lesson.
