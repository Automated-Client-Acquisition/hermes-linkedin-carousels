# Pattern: observation

A sharp take on a trend or pattern the user is seeing across multiple
cases. The post names the pattern, lists what it looks like, and ends
with the unflattering conclusion.

## When to use

- The user has seen the pattern repeatedly and can name it precisely.
- The audience is positioned to either be guilty of the pattern or
  recognize it in others.
- The hook is `bold-claim`, `contrarian-punch`, or
  `pattern-interrupt-question`.

## Structure

```
What you're seeing (1-2 lines): name the pattern.

Three or four short specifics (lines, no bullets needed):
what the pattern looks like in practice.

The unflattering conclusion (1-2 lines): what the pattern actually means.

The instruction or warning (1 line, optional).
```

## Worked example

```
I'm watching agencies panic-add "AI" to their service pages.

No new capabilities.
No new workflows.
Just a buzzword bolted onto the same deliverables.

Your clients aren't stupid.
They can tell the difference between AI-native and AI-labeled.
```

## Rules

- Name the pattern in one line. "I'm watching X" or "Everywhere I look,
  Y" both work.
- The specifics are short. One thing per line.
- The conclusion is the punchline. Save it for last.
- 3-4 specifics is the sweet spot. Two reads weak; five reads padded.
- Real observations only. If the user hasn't seen this happen, don't
  manufacture the pattern.

## What NOT to do

- Don't name a specific company in the observation unless the user is
  willing to back it publicly.
- Don't pile on. The pattern speaks for itself; sneering on top reads as
  bitter.
- Don't end on a question. The observation lands harder as a flat
  conclusion.
- Don't generalize to "everyone": calibrate to "I'm watching agencies"
  or "I'm seeing operators". The narrower the audience, the sharper the
  observation.

## Pairs well with

Hooks: `bold-claim`, `contrarian-punch`, `pattern-interrupt-question`.
CTAs: `mic-drop` (the conclusion IS the mic drop) or `bridge`.
