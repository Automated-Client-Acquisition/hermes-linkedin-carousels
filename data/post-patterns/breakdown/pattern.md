# Pattern: breakdown

Deconstruct a process, framework, or result step by step. The post is a
mini case study. Setup line names the result. Arrow lines walk through
what produced it. Closing line ties it back to the why.

## When to use

- The user has a specific result and a specific list of moves that
  produced it.
- The audience benefits from operational detail, not philosophy.
- The hook is `bold-claim`, `specific-number`, `bold-number`, or
  `credibility`.
- The voice is any of the three shipped voices.

## Structure

```
Setup: a line naming the result and what you'll explain.
→ Move 1 (one specific action)
→ Move 2 (one specific action)
→ Move 3 (one specific action)
(3-5 moves total)

Outcome: the metric or change that resulted.

Reframe: a short line on WHY this worked.
```

## Worked example

```
Here's what actually moved the needle:

→ We rewrote the offer positioning (not the email copy)
→ Switched from generic to signal-based targeting
→ Cut the sequence from 7 steps to 3

Response rate went from 1.2% to 8.7%.

Not because of "better writing."
Because of better thinking.
```

## Rules

- Each arrow is ONE move. If a line needs "and", split it.
- 3-5 arrows max. Six is too many. Two is not a breakdown.
- The outcome line is concrete. "Response rate went from X to Y" beats
  "things improved."
- The reframe line is short. One or two sentences. Never a paragraph.
- Real numbers only. If you don't have the metric, drop the outcome line
  and end on the reframe.

## What NOT to do

- Don't number the arrows ("1. We did..."). The `→` glyph IS the
  numbering.
- Don't pad arrows with adjectives. "We strategically optimized our
  positioning" is filler. "We rewrote the offer" is a move.
- Don't reveal the reframe before the arrows. The arrows earn it.
- Don't end with a CTA-bridge sentence ("if you want to know more, DM
  me"). The CTA is a separate section after the body.

## Pairs well with

Hooks: `bold-claim`, `specific-number`, `bold-number`, `credibility`,
`contrarian-punch`. CTAs: `keyword-capture` (the full breakdown in a doc)
or `mic-drop` (the reframe line IS the mic drop).
