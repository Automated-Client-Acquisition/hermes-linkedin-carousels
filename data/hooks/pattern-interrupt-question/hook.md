# Hook: pattern-interrupt-question

A question the reader has not considered. The question implies that the
reader's existing assumption is wrong without naming it. Line 2 sharpens
the question's stakes.

## Formula

```
Line 1: "What if [thing the audience takes for granted] is wrong?"
Line 2: "Not slightly off. [Stronger version of the implication.]"
```

## When to use

- The post body actually answers the question. A question hook without a
  payoff burns trust.
- The audience genuinely takes the named thing for granted.
- Voice is `hype` or `senior-operator`. `clean` voice can use it but
  drops the "Not slightly off" line, which removes most of the energy.

## Worked examples

```
What if your ICP is wrong?
Not slightly off. Completely, fundamentally wrong.
```

```
What if the channel is fine and the offer is the problem?
Not a small problem. The reason the whole pipeline is dry.
```

```
What if "sales cycle length" isn't the metric to manage?
The number is real. The mental model around it is what's broken.
```

## Common failure modes

- The question is rhetorical and obvious. "What if you could grow your
  business?" is not a hook.
- The post body doesn't answer the question.
- The reader can answer the question in their head and feel smarter for
  it. The hook has to leave them genuinely unsettled.
- The hook stops being a question and turns into a polite essay opener.
  No "Have you ever wondered..."

## Pairs well with

`reframe` post pattern (the body provides the new mental model that the
question pointed at) or `observation` (the body shows the evidence).
CTAs: `mic-drop`, `bridge`, or `keyword-capture`.
