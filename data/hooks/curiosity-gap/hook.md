# Hook: curiosity-gap

Open a loop the reader must close. The classic Brunson curiosity hook.
Line 1 announces something specific that happened. Line 2 promises that
the result rewrites a held belief, without revealing what the result is.

## Formula

```
Line 1: [I/We did a specific concrete thing at scale.]
Line 2: [The result changed how I think about X.]
```

## When to use

- You have real data or a real experience to back up the curiosity.
- The post body actually closes the loop (curiosity hooks that never
  resolve burn trust fast).
- The voice is `hype` or `senior-operator`. Less suited to `clean`,
  which doesn't traffic in dramatic reveals.

## Worked examples

```
I sent 10,000 cold emails last month.
The results broke everything I thought I knew.
```

```
We ran [N] campaigns across [N] accounts.
The pattern that emerged was not what we expected.
```

```
I tested [the obvious thing] for six weeks.
The data made me change the entire approach.
```

## Common failure modes

- The loop never closes. The post body must deliver the surprise.
- The "specific thing" is too generic. "I tried something new" is not a
  hook. "I sent 10,000 cold emails" is.
- The reveal in the post body is underwhelming. Calibrate the hook to
  the size of the actual insight.
- Fake numbers. Truth rule overrides this entire archetype. If you
  didn't send 10,000, don't say 10,000.

## Pairs well with

`story` post pattern (narrative payoff matches narrative hook) or
`system-reveal` (the specific thing IS the system). CTAs:
`keyword-capture` (the doc closes the loop) or `mic-drop`.
