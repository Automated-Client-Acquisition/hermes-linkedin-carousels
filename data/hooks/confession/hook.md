# Hook: confession

Admit something unexpected. The vulnerability is the hook. Line 1 names
a loss or a mistake. Line 2 reframes it as a turning point or a lesson.

## Formula

```
Line 1: [A specific loss, failure, or admission, dated or scoped concretely.]
Line 2: [What that loss / failure actually unlocked or taught.]
```

## When to use

- The user has a real story to tell. This archetype lives or dies on
  authenticity.
- The lesson learned is genuinely useful, not just self-congratulation.
- Voice is `hype` or `clean`. `senior-operator` rarely confesses; the
  voice doesn't have the warmth.

## Worked examples

```
I lost my biggest client last Tuesday.
It was the best thing that happened to my business.
```

```
We shipped the wrong product for the first six months.
The pivot that fixed it took 90 minutes.
```

```
I rebuilt the same broken sequence three times before I admitted what was wrong.
It was not the sequence.
```

## Common failure modes

- **Fabrication is fatal.** Truth rule applies hardest here. If you
  didn't lose a client last Tuesday, do not write that you did. Readers
  who catch you never come back.
- The "lesson" is generic ("I learned to be resilient"). The lesson has
  to be specific and operational.
- The confession is humble-brag in disguise ("I almost made too much
  money too fast"). Reads worse than no confession.
- The story is too long for a hook. Line 1 must be one event. Save the
  detail for the body.

## Pairs well with

`story` post pattern (the body is the rest of the narrative). CTAs:
`mic-drop` or `bridge`. Avoid `keyword-capture` on confession posts —
the tonal shift to "comment a word" undercuts the vulnerability.
