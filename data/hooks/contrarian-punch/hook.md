# Hook: contrarian-punch

Challenge something the audience believes. Line 1 states the common wisdom
as if quoting it. Line 2 corrects it sharply. The tension between the two
lines is the hook.

## Formula

```
Line 1: "Most [people in audience] think [common belief]."
Line 2: [Direct correction or reframe.]
```

Or the inverted form:

```
Line 1: [Common belief stated as flat fact.]
Line 2: Wrong. [The real cause / what actually matters.]
```

## When to use

- The source material has a clear contrarian thesis.
- The audience holds a specific belief you can name precisely.
- The post body delivers the actual answer (a contrarian hook with no
  follow-through reads as clickbait).
- The voice is `senior-operator`, `hype`, or `clean` — all three carry it.

## Worked examples

```
Most cold emails fail because of the subject line.
Wrong. The subject line is the least of your problems.
```

```
Most operators think reply rate is the goal.
It is not. Booked meetings per 100 accounts is.
```

```
Everyone optimizes the template.
The template is the smallest variable in the system.
```

## Common failure modes

- The contrarian claim is fake. If you don't actually disagree, the hook
  is theater. Readers can tell.
- The correction in line 2 is vague. "It's more complicated than that" is
  not a hook.
- The audience doesn't actually hold the belief you're challenging.
  Strawman hooks read as condescending.
- Line 1 is too long. The "most people think" frame eats characters fast;
  keep the belief short.

## Pairs well with

Pairs naturally with the `reframe` post pattern (the post body proves the
correction) or the `breakdown` pattern (the post body shows the system
that makes the contrarian view true). CTAs: `mic-drop` or `keyword-capture`.
