# Hook: curiosity-gap-misdirect

Misdirect away from the obvious cause. Line 1 names a painful problem
and dismisses the assumed cause. Line 2 promises the real reason. The
gap between "obvious wrong answer" and "real answer" is the hook.

## Formula

```
Line 1: "The reason [painful problem] has nothing to do with [obvious assumed cause]."
Line 2: "It is this."
```

## When to use

- The audience reliably blames the wrong thing for a known problem.
- You actually know the real cause and the post body delivers it.
- The voice is `hype` or `senior-operator`. The "it is this" line lands
  hardest in those voices.

## Worked examples

```
The reason your LinkedIn pipeline is dry has nothing to do with your content.
It is this.
```

```
The reason your cold email reply rate is flat has nothing to do with your template.
It is this.
```

```
The reason your hire keeps quitting has nothing to do with the salary.
It is this.
```

## Common failure modes

- The "real reason" in the post body is just the obvious cause restated.
  That's a bait-and-switch and readers spot it.
- The hook is too vague about which painful problem. "Why your career is
  stuck" is too broad; "why your last 3 raise requests got nothing" is a
  hook.
- Line 2 ("It is this.") gets dropped or softened. The flat assertion is
  the energy. "Here's what I think might be going on" kills the hook.

## Pairs well with

`reframe` post pattern (the body teaches the new mental model) or
`observation` pattern (the body names a pattern the audience hasn't seen).
CTAs: `keyword-capture` or `mic-drop`.
