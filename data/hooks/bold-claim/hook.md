# Hook: bold-claim

A declarative statement that demands attention. No question, no hedge,
no setup. Just the claim, stated flat. Line 2 sharpens it.

## Formula

```
Line 1: [Bold flat statement.]
Line 2: [A consequence, qualification, or sharper version of line 1.]
```

## When to use

- The user has a strong, defensible point of view and can back it.
- The claim is provocative without being a strawman.
- Voice is `hype` (energy) or `senior-operator` (cold confidence). Less
  suited to `clean`, which softens flat claims by default.

## Worked examples

```
AI won't replace agencies.
Bad agencies just won't survive 2026.
```

```
The next five years of cold email belong to the operators who can read a calendar.
Not the ones who write better subject lines.
```

```
Most of the SDR industry will be gone by 2027.
The work won't disappear. The job title will.
```

## Common failure modes

- The claim is too soft. "AI is changing everything" is not a claim. "AI
  won't replace agencies" is.
- Line 2 retreats. The second line should sharpen, not qualify. "Of
  course it depends on..." kills the hook.
- The user can't actually back the claim. Confidence without substance
  is recognizable.
- The claim is so contrarian it reads as posturing. There's a fine line
  between bold and clout-chasing.

## Pairs well with

`observation` post pattern (you back the claim with a pattern you've
seen) or `breakdown` (you back it with the system). CTAs: `mic-drop` or
`bridge`.
