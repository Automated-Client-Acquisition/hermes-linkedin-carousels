# Hook: stop

Command the reader to halt a common behavior. Capital "STOP" stops the
scroll. Line 2 names what to do instead and previews the payoff.

## Formula

```
Line 1: "STOP [doing the thing everyone does]."
Line 2: "[Other method] just [desired outcome]."
```

## When to use

- The "doing the thing everyone does" is precise and recognizable.
- The "other method" is something the post body actually teaches.
- Voice is `hype` (strongest fit) or `senior-operator` (cooler version,
  drops the capital STOP).

## Worked examples

```
STOP using GPT for your LinkedIn content.
Claude just generated us 1M+ impressions in 7 days.
```

```
STOP rewriting your cold email templates.
The template is a 5% lever.
```

```
STOP buying scraped lists.
Trigger-built lists reply at 10x the rate.
```

## Common failure modes

- The behavior named in line 1 is too narrow or too specific to one
  niche. "STOP using FormulaBot for emails" doesn't land if 90% of the
  audience has never used FormulaBot.
- The "other method" in line 2 is vague. "Try a different approach"
  isn't a payoff.
- Capital STOP gets repeated inside the post body. The shouting belongs
  only on line 1.
- Two STOP hooks in two weeks reads as scolding.

## Pairs well with

`breakdown` post pattern (the body walks through the "other method") or
`reframe` (the body explains why the common behavior was always wrong).
CTAs: `keyword-capture` (the system in a doc).
