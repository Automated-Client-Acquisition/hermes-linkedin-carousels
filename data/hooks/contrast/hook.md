# Hook: contrast

Juxtapose a painful before with an exciting after. The gap between the
two states is the hook. Line 1 is the painful past. Line 2 is the
specific better present.

## Formula

```
Line 1: "[Timeframe] ago: [painful before state]."
Line 2: "Today: [specific exciting after state]."
```

## When to use

- The transformation is real and dated specifically. "Last quarter" is
  better than "a while back".
- The "after" is concrete and measurable. "Things got better" doesn't
  contrast.
- Voice is `hype` or `clean`. Both carry it; `clean` does it without the
  drama, `hype` adds the energy.

## Worked examples

```
[N] days ago this founder had zero sales calls.
Today he books [N] high-intent calls every single day.
```

```
[Timeframe] ago: writing every cold email by hand.
Today: a research-first system that drafts and ships them.
```

```
[Timeframe] ago: 0.4% reply rate from a 50,000-name list.
Today: 4% reply rate from a list of 500.
```

## Common failure modes

- The before and after aren't comparable. Comparing "miserable" to "12
  meetings booked" doesn't work because the units don't match.
- The timeframe is vague. "A while back" kills the specificity.
- The after sounds too good. Calibrate to plausible numbers; readers
  reject results that feel inflated.
- Fake transformation. Truth rule applies hardest here — if the user
  isn't the "this founder," frame it as "a client" and only if the
  client gave permission.

## Pairs well with

`story` post pattern (the body tells the transformation) or
`system-reveal` (the body explains what changed). CTAs: `mic-drop` or
`keyword-capture` (the system that produced the change).
