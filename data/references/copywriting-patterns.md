# Carousel Copywriting Patterns

Reference for STEP 2 (script). Use these as starting structures, not
fill-in-the-blank templates — the voice chosen for the run still drives the
actual wording.

Core rule: **one clear point per slide.** If a slide needs "and", it's two
slides.

---

## Slide 1 — Hook formulas

The first slide decides whether the carousel gets read. It must work as a
thumbnail. Pick the formula that fits the source's strongest insight:

1. **The contrarian** — "Most people get [topic] backwards." / "[Common
   advice] is quietly costing you [outcome]."
2. **The number** — "[N]% of [group] [surprising stat]." / "I analyzed [N]
   [things]. Here's the pattern."
3. **The sharp question** — "Why does [expected thing] keep [failing]?" — only
   if the carousel actually answers it.
4. **The stakes** — "If you [do common thing], you're leaving [specific value]
   on the table."
5. **The promise** — "[N] [things] that [specific outcome] — in [timeframe]."
6. **The reframe** — "[Topic] isn't a [assumed category] problem. It's a
   [real category] problem."

Hook anti-patterns: no "Let's dive in", no "In today's fast-paced world", no
throat-clearing, no question the carousel doesn't answer.

---

## Body slides — structure

Each body slide = **one insight from `source/insights.md`**, expressed as:

- **Headline** — 3–7 words. The point itself, not a label. ("Speed beats
  polish" — not "Point 3: Speed".)
- **Body** — one supporting line. The evidence, the mechanism, or the example.
- **Visual note** — concrete brief for the image.

Narrative thread: each slide should make the next one feel earned. Common
arcs:
- **Problem → cause → mechanism → fix → proof → CTA**
- **Myth → why it persists → what's true → what to do → CTA**
- **List arc** — N parallel points, escalating in strength, strongest last.

Keep the deck honest: respect what the audience already believes (from
`brand.md`), don't strawman the "wrong" view.

---

## Final slide — Payoff + CTA

Two beats on one slide:
1. **Payoff** — the takeaway lands in one line. The thing they should
   remember.
2. **CTA** — exactly one ask.

### CTA formats

- **Save** — "Save this for the next time you [situation]." Best for
  reference/checklist content.
- **Follow** — "Follow [handle] for more on [specific niche]." Best for
  audience growth; name the niche, not "more content".
- **Comment a word** — "Comment **[WORD]** and I'll send you [specific
  thing]." Best for lead generation. Rules for the trigger word:
  - ONE word, uppercase, on-topic, easy to spell.
  - It maps to a ManyChat-style auto-DM, so it must be exact and memorable.
  - The thing offered must be specific and real (a named lead magnet, not
    "more info").

Record the trigger word in the workspace file (`comment_trigger_word`) so the
export checklist can surface it.

### CTA anti-patterns
No stacked asks ("like, comment, share, and follow"). No vague "let me know
your thoughts". No CTA that wasn't set up by the carousel's content.

---

## Post copy (the caption above the carousel)

Separate from the slides. 3–6 short lines:
- Line 1 — a hook that complements (doesn't repeat) slide 1.
- Middle — 1–3 lines of context or a sharper version of the promise.
- Last line — a soft nudge to swipe ("The breakdown's in the carousel 👇" —
  match emoji use to brand voice; some brands use none).

Front-load it — LinkedIn and Instagram both truncate after a couple of lines.
